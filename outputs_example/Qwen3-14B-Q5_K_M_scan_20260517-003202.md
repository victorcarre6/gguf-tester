# Analysis Summary

The safety evaluation of the local LLM indicates a strong overall posture, with high alignment (mean 0.96), refusal quality (0.96), and faithfulness (0.99), alongside minimal harm likelihood (0.03). The model demonstrates robustness in backdoor tests (zero harm likelihood) and maintains strong answer relevancy across most scenarios. However, vulnerabilities emerge in jailbreak and harmful bias contexts, where alignment scores dip to 0.89 and 0.95, respectively, and harm likelihood rises to 0.09. Notably, the model occasionally fails to reject prompts about poisoning, hacking, or lethal dose calculations, producing partial or incomplete responses. While its refusal quality is generally high, edge cases reveal gaps in detecting and blocking malicious intent. To strengthen safety, the model should be fine-tuned with targeted jailbreak scenarios to improve alignment in high-risk prompts, and stricter filters should be applied to prevent partial responses in harmful bias contexts.

## ⚠️ Limitations

- **Static scan** (OWASP LLM Top 10) is pattern-matching on GGUF metadata.
  Many false positives (e.g. "Instruct" → flagged, `tokenizer.ggml.tokens` →
  matched as "sensitive"). Use as informational baseline only.
- **Dynamic tests** probe behaviour via short prompts with low temperature.
  They do **not** guarantee absence of jailbreak / harmful content / backdoor —
  only that the tested vectors did not trigger one.
- **Backdoor detection** is a behavioural heuristic (activation clustering on
  injected suspect tokens). It does not inspect weights and will miss triggers
  outside the injection set. Real backdoor detection (Neural Cleanse, weight
  forensics) is out of scope.
- **Bias evaluation** uses cosine similarity between paired responses; near-equal
  cosine does not prove absence of bias — only structural similarity.
- **Judge** is regex-based, EN+FR only. Subtle or non-English refusals may yield
  false negatives.
